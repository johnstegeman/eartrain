## Problem Statement

No app exists for practicing guitar as a cochlear implant (CI) user. Every existing CI music app
treats users as passive listeners reconnecting with music after implantation. This user is an active
guitarist who wants practice feedback adapted to CI-specific perceptual challenges. The rehabilitation
world built for listeners. The instrument-practice world ignores CI users entirely.

## What Makes This Cool

A practicing guitarist with cochlear implants has a fundamentally different feedback loop than a
hearing guitarist. The standard tools give you nothing useful: tuner apps tell you whether you're in
tune, but can't tell you whether you played the right interval. Ear training apps assume you hear
what they expect you to hear. This app closes the loop honestly — it plays something, listens to
what you played, and tells you the truth. Over time it builds a confusion map specific to YOU: which
intervals blur together at which registers, so it can drill exactly the gaps that your CI creates.

The CI-specific streaming insight is a small detail that reveals a lot: CI users who stream Mac audio
directly to their processor via Bluetooth face a connection activation delay whenever audio starts
playing. The workaround is to run a silent YouTube video in the background. No existing app handles
this. This one will, from day one.

## Target User

Active guitarist with cochlear implants. Not a beginner, not in rehab — someone who already plays
and wants to improve. Practices at a desk or guitar stand in front of their Mac. Uses a mic or audio
interface to capture guitar output. May stream Mac audio directly to their CI via Bluetooth.

## Constraints

- Mac desktop app (primary platform)
- Input: microphone or audio interface
- No timbre discrimination required (not comparing instruments, just pitches)
- CI Bluetooth streaming: app must output a constant inaudible signal to prevent stream activation delay
- Must feel like a practice tool, not a rehabilitation app

## Premises

1. The core value is the feedback loop: hear → play → compare. Everything else (melody echo,
   blues licks, improv) is built on top of this same loop.
2. The app doesn't need to model CI perception internally. It needs accurate pitch detection and
   honest comparison. CI-specific value is in exercise design: which intervals to drill, register
   awareness, confusion tracking. (Additional CI-adaptive drill logic is welcome if achievable.)
3. Mac desktop app. Guitar + mic/audio interface. Sitting at your instrument in front of your Mac.
4. MVP = interval trainer with CI-specific drill logic. Melody echo comes second.

## CI-Specific Interval Challenges (from user)

- Major/minor thirds (m3 vs M3) sound the same or very similar
- Perfect fifth (P5) sometimes sounds like an octave (P8)
- Register-dependent: higher and lower pitch ranges affect confusion patterns differently
- NOT a timbre/instrument discrimination problem
- Interval set: all intervals present in major and minor pentatonic scales
  - M2 (2 semitones), m3 (3), M3 (4), P4 (5), P5 (7), M6 (9), m7 (10), P8 (12)
  - Priority drill set (highest confusion): m3, M3, P5, P8

## Research Foundation

Key findings from published CI music perception research and their implications for training design.

### Individual variation is massive — calibration is mandatory

CI users span an enormous performance range. Best performers identify intervals correctly at >90%
with 2-semitone gaps; worst performers score <40% with 5-semitone gaps (Frontiers 2022). You
cannot assume any starting point. The Phase 0 onboarding assessment is directly supported by this
finding — the app must measure each user before assigning drills.

**Implication:** Calibration isn't a nice-to-have. It's the mechanism that makes the app useful
for anyone other than one specific user.

### Adaptive drilling outperforms fixed curricula

The 2022 Frontiers study showing only "small effect" from interval training used a *fixed*
curriculum — the same intervals in the same order for every user. Perceptual learning research
(PMC 2013) consistently shows adaptive approaches that target individual weak spots produce
larger and faster gains. "Drill My Misses" is this approach.

**Implication:** The confusion matrix + adaptive drill selection is the core scientific
differentiator from every existing CI music app.

### Start wide, narrow progressively

Research consistently uses melodic contour (up/down) as the easiest baseline task, then
progressively narrows interval size. Large gaps (P8 vs M2, 10+ semitones) are reliably easier
for CI users than small gaps (m3 vs M3, 1 semitone). Starting at the wrong difficulty
discourages or under-challenges.

**Implication:** The onboarding assessment should progress from maximally different intervals
toward similar-sounding pairs. The drill set ordering within a session should do the same.

### Pure sine tones are the right training stimulus

Published CI training studies use pure tones to eliminate timbre as a variable and isolate
frequency discrimination. Guitar samples introduce harmonic complexity that confounds the
interval detection task during training (though guitar playback is fine for real practice).

**Implication:** App plays intervals as sine tones (SineOscillator). Guitar recordings or
sampled instrument sounds would make the training task harder without making it more useful.

### 12 hours of training produces measurable improvement

Rate discrimination improves with as little as 12 hours of focused auditory training (PLOS ONE
2020). A randomized crossover study (Springer/JARO 2019) found average music comprehension
improvement of 28.3 percentage points after training (range: 15.5–45.4 pp).

**Implication:** Progress tracking is motivationally and scientifically important. The app
should surface cumulative session time and accuracy trends so users can see whether training
is working. A "12-hour milestone" marker in the progress view would be meaningful.

### Register matters via frequency-to-place mapping

A 2025 Frontiers paper shows that individual differences in CI electrode insertion depth create
unique frequency-to-place maps — each user's CI maps frequencies to electrodes slightly
differently based on their cochlear anatomy and surgical insertion. This is why register affects
interval perception differently for different users.

**Implication:** Register bucketing (low/mid/high) in the confusion matrix captures a real
perceptual phenomenon, not just a UI convenience. Long-term: the pattern of register-dependent
confusions in the confusion matrix is essentially a fingerprint of the user's frequency-to-place
mapping. Future versions could use this data to characterize the user's individual CI map.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Native SwiftUI + AudioKit (CHOSEN)
Full native Mac app. SwiftUI UI, AudioKit for mic input via PitchTap, AVFoundation for interval
playback, Core Data or simple JSON for confusion matrix.
- Effort: M (1-2 weekends)
- Risk: Low
- Pros: native performance, low-latency mic input, AudioKit is the standard for music on macOS,
  confusion matrix + "Drill My Misses" straightforward to implement
- Cons: Swift/SwiftUI learning curve if new to the stack; occasional AppKit interop friction

### Approach B: Web App / Electron
Browser or Electron app using Web Audio API + pitchy.js. Cross-platform, easy to share as URL.
- Effort: S (1 weekend)
- Risk: Medium
- Cons: 10-50ms higher latency than native; pitch detection less robust for guitar; Bluetooth
  keep-alive harder to manage in a browser context

### Approach C: Python Prototype
Python + aubio/librosa + pyaudio. Fastest path to testing the core loop.
- Effort: S (hours)
- Risk: Low (prototype) / High (if you want it to feel like a real app)
- Cons: won't feel like a native Mac app; would need rewrite before sharing

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A: Native SwiftUI + AudioKit.**

AudioKit's `PitchTap` node handles real-time pitch detection from mic input with musical accuracy.
AVFoundation handles interval playback (synthesized tones or sampled notes). SwiftUI gives a clean
native UI. The confusion matrix is just a 2D dictionary: `[Interval: [Register: [Result]]]` — log
every trial and summarize on demand.

The Bluetooth keep-alive is one `AVAudioEngine` output node playing a 0-amplitude buffer on a
background queue. Toggleable in Settings.

## Feature Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Onboarding Assessment (first launch only)

New users do a short diagnostic before any drills. Goal: seed the confusion matrix with enough
data to start "Drill My Misses" immediately and avoid starting at the wrong difficulty.

- **Listening-only mode**: App plays interval pairs, user clicks which one they heard (no guitar
  needed). Faster to complete, removes the guitar input variable from calibration.
- Covers the 8 pentatonic intervals across 3 register buckets = 24 buckets total
- Short form: 2 trials per bucket = ~48 questions, ~5-8 minutes
- Results: confusion matrix pre-seeded; easy buckets deprioritized; hard ones surfaced first
- Progressive difficulty: starts with maximally different intervals (P8 vs M2) and works toward
  similar-sounding pairs (m3 vs M3, P5 vs P8)
- User can skip and start from scratch (app will just need more trials before Drill My Misses kicks in)
- Assessment runs once at first launch; can be re-run from Settings ("Reset & recalibrate")

### Phase 0 — Onboarding completion state
After the 48-question calibration, show a "Here's what we learned" summary screen with the
top 2-3 identified confusion patterns (e.g., "Your CI tends to blur m3 and M3 in the low
register"). Then: "Start your first session" CTA. Makes the calibration feel meaningful.

### Phase 1 — MVP: Interval Trainer
- App plays two notes (root + interval) using pure sine waves (cleaner for CI perception)
- User plays the interval back on guitar via mic/audio interface
- `AmplitudeTracker` (AudioKit) detects note onsets; `PitchTap` detects pitch per onset
- Pitch grading — four result cases (evaluated in priority order):
  1. `octaveDisplaced` — interval class matches mod 12, within ±50 cents of octave-shifted target
     (e.g., played P5 an octave up; detection rule: `abs(detected_semitones - target_semitones) % 12 < 0.5`)
     (checked first to prevent boundary misclassification)
  2. `correct` — detected pitch within ±25 cents of target
  3. `close(played: Interval)` — within ±100 cents (1 semitone) but outside ±25 cents (catches m3/M3 confusion)
  4. `wrong(played: Interval)` — more than 1 semitone off; shows what was played
  - If PitchTap confidence < 0.9 for 10+ consecutive frames after onset: no Result logged; user prompted to replay
- All four cases (and no-read events) logged in confusion matrix per (interval, register) bucket
- "Root note" in Settings is fixed for the session (user-selected before starting); not randomized
  per exercise. This keeps the exercise context stable and simplifies session setup.
- Interval set: M2, m3, M3, P4, P5, M6, m7, P8 (all pentatonic scale intervals)
- Register buckets (by guitar fretboard range):
  - Low: E2–B3 (open strings to 4th string 9th fret)
  - Mid: C4–B4 (roughly 5th position area)
  - High: C5 and above
- Confusion matrix: logs `(interval, register) → [Result]` for every attempt
- Progress tracking: session-by-session accuracy per interval and register; trend line
  visible in Progress view so improvement over time is clearly visible
- "Drill My Misses": activates after ≥5 trials per bucket; ranks buckets by error rate;
  next session weighted toward worst-performing interval+register combinations
- CI keep-alive: `AVAudioEngine` output node playing 0-amplitude buffer continuously,
  user-toggleable in Settings, on by default
- Data persistence: JSON files in app support directory (one file per session + cumulative)
- Settings: root note, active interval set, register range, input device selection,
  CI keep-alive toggle, fretboard hint display (on/off + detail level), pitch tolerance,
  audio buffer size (Advanced), feedback delay (correct: 2s, wrong: 4s, configurable)

### Phase 1 — Interaction Design (hands-free practice loop)

**Core loop is fully hands-free. User holds guitar throughout a session.**

- **Input gate**: pitch detection is disabled while the interval tone is playing. Detection
  resumes 500ms after playback ends to prevent the sine tone from self-triggering.
- **Feedback delivery**:
  1. App plays interval as sine tones (user taps ▶ or presses Return)
  2. User plays the interval on guitar
  3. Gate approach: onset detected → N=3 PitchTap frames stable → grade note
  4. Audio cue plays immediately through CI stream:
     - Correct: ascending major triad (short, pleasant)
     - Close: single neutral tone
     - Wrong: descending minor second (distinctive, not punishing)
     - OctaveDisplaced: correct tone pattern but pitch-shifted down one octave
  5. Inline feedback appears showing result + what was played
  6. App auto-advances after adaptive delay: 2s (correct/close) or 4s (wrong/octaveDisplaced)
  7. Spacebar skips the delay and advances immediately (keyboard users)
- **Wrong feedback UX**: on `wrong` and `octaveDisplaced` results, the fretboard shows
  BOTH the target note and what was detected — two highlighted dots, different colors.
  Gives spatial/kinesthetic reference alongside the audio explanation.
- **Audio interruptions** (FaceTime, notification, other app): session pauses, banner
  displays "Audio interrupted — press Return to resume." Session state preserved, no data
  lost. User resumes explicitly.
- **Session pause**: Escape key pauses the session. Same banner UI as interruption.

### Phase 1 — Fretboard Position Hints

- **Optional**: toggled on/off in Settings and in ExerciseView header (quick access)
- **Detail levels** (configurable in Settings):
  - "Primary position only": shows one optimal fret position for the target note
  - "All positions": shows the primary position + 1-2 alternate positions (dashed outline)
  - "Off": fretboard hidden
- **When shown**: displays root note (blue dot) and target note (amber dot) with finger
  numbers. Alternate positions shown in dashed amber at reduced opacity.
- **Progression logic**: future versions can auto-reduce hints as accuracy improves in a
  bucket (e.g., turn off after 3 consecutive correct in the same bucket). Not in Phase 1.

### Phase 1 — Session Duration

Users choose how long they want to practice before each session starts. This sets pacing
expectations and keeps sessions from feeling open-ended.

**Session Start Sheet** (modal presented when tapping "Start Session" from HomeView):
- Duration grid: **5 min** (~15 exercises) / **10 min** (~30 exercises) / **20 min** (~60 exercises) / **30 min** (~90 exercises) / **Custom** (free-entry in minutes)
- Approximate exercise counts based on ~2-3 minutes average per exercise (including playback + response + feedback delay)
- Pre-selects the last-used duration
- Mode toggle: **Standard** (random from active interval set) / **Drill My Misses** (weighted toward weak buckets; greyed out with tooltip if fewer than 5 trials per bucket)
- Quick settings summary row showing active root note, interval set, and register range — tap to open Settings without closing the sheet
- "Start" button launches ExerciseView with session_duration set

**During session (ExerciseView)**:
- Time badge in header: "14:32 remaining" — updates every second
- Progress bar below header: fills left-to-right as time elapses
- No hard stop at time limit: if an exercise is in progress when time expires, it completes naturally
- At time limit: "Session complete" banner appears; user can finish current exercise then tap "End Session" or keep going

**End of Session screen** (shown after last exercise when time has expired, or when user taps "End Session"):
- Stats grid: accuracy this session / delta vs. previous session (↑↓ with color) / total practice time cumulative
- "What this session revealed" panel: 2-3 highest-error buckets from this session only (e.g., "m3 in the low register: 3/8 correct")
- CTA buttons: "View Progress" (ProgressView) / "Start Another Session" (back to HomeView)
- Session auto-saved before screen appears; data visible in ProgressView immediately

**Persistence**: `session_duration` and last-selected `mode` stored in `cumulative.json` preferences block (alongside root note, interval set). Pre-selected on next launch.

### Phase 2 — Melody Echo
- App plays a short melody (3-8 notes) in a chosen key
- User plays it back on guitar
- Note-by-note comparison: which notes were right, which were wrong, which were close
- Builds on the same pitch detection + register bucketing from Phase 1

## Technical Architecture

**Platform targets:** macOS 13 (Ventura) minimum. Swift 5.9+. AudioKit 5.x via Swift Package Manager.

```
EarTrain (macOS SwiftUI app)
├── Audio Engine (AudioKit — single AVAudioEngine instance)
│   ├── AmplitudeTracker — RMS amplitude gate for onset detection
│   ├── PitchTap — continuous pitch polling (fundamental frequency, ~93ms/frame)
│   ├── SineOscillator — interval/melody playback (pure tones, better for CI)
│   └── Silence mixer node — 0-amplitude Mixer in AudioKit graph for CI Bluetooth keep-alive
│       (NOT a separate AVAudioEngine — AudioKit owns the engine; silence node lives in its graph)
├── Note Detection Pipeline (gate approach)
│   ├── Onset: AmplitudeTracker crosses threshold (-40dBFS → -20dBFS) → start collecting PitchTap readings
│   ├── Stability: N=3 consecutive PitchTap readings within 25 cents → lock pitch, grade note
│   ├── Reset: amplitude drops below onset threshold → reset gate, ready for next note
│   ├── Confidence: PitchTap confidence < 0.9 → discard frame (don't count toward N=3)
│   └── No-read: if confidence < 0.9 for 10+ consecutive frames → show "couldn't detect pitch, try again"
├── Exercise Engine
│   ├── IntervalExercise — generates root + target interval, evaluates response
│   │   ├── Result enum (priority order — check in this sequence):
│   │   │   1. octaveDisplaced — interval class matches mod 12, within ±50 cents of octave-shifted target
│   │   │   2. correct — within ±25 cents of target
│   │   │   3. close(played: Interval) — within ±100 cents (1 semitone) of target
│   │   │   4. wrong(played: Interval) — all other cases
│   │   └── (octaveDisplaced checked first to prevent misclassification at the ±1 semitone boundary)
│   └── RegisterBucket — maps Hz to low/mid/high (E2–B3 / C4–B4 / C5+)
│       (MelodyExercise is Phase 2 — not in Phase 1 codebase)
├── State Management
│   ├── AudioKit callbacks arrive on background threads
│   ├── All UI state updates dispatched to @MainActor via DispatchQueue.main.async or AsyncStream
│   └── ExerciseViewModel (@MainActor ObservableObject) owns current exercise state, last Result
├── Analytics
│   ├── ConfusionMatrix — [Interval: [Register: [Result]]] per session + cumulative
│   ├── ProgressStore — session history, accuracy trends over time
│   └── DrillMisses — ranks buckets by error rate (min 5 trials), generates drill set
├── Persistence
│   └── JSON files in ~/Library/Application Support/EarTrain/
│       ├── sessions/ — one file per practice session (includes schemaVersion: Int)
│       └── cumulative.json — aggregated confusion matrix + progress (includes schemaVersion: Int)
│           (schema versioning added now; migration needed if Result enum gains new cases)
└── UI (SwiftUI) — 7 screens
    ├── HomeView — start session, quick stats, "Drill My Misses" shortcut
    ├── SessionStartSheet — duration picker + mode toggle (modal over HomeView)
    ├── ExerciseView — active practice: play interval, listen, get feedback
    ├── FeedbackView — per-attempt result (modal/inline after each response)
    ├── EndOfSessionView — session stats + insights + CTAs (shown at session end)
    ├── ProgressView — confusion matrix heatmap + accuracy trend over time
    └── SettingsView — input device, interval set, register range, keep-alive toggle,
                       audio buffer size (128/256/512 samples — Advanced section)
```

## Open Questions

1. **Pitch detection accuracy on low guitar strings**: Guitar has complex harmonics. `PitchTap`
   can be confused by strong overtones on low strings (E2, A2). Mitigation: set a minimum
   confidence threshold (e.g., 0.9) and require pitch stability across N consecutive frames
   before confirming a note. If this proves unreliable, `aubio` (via a Swift bridge or a
   lightweight Python sidecar) is a fallback with better guitar-optimized fundamental extraction.
2. **Bent notes**: Pitch bending produces a continuous glide. For Phase 1 (interval training),
   bending isn't required — the user plays discrete notes. For Phase 3+ (blues licks), the app
   will need to detect the peak pitch of a bend or track the pitch contour. Deferred to Phase 3.
3. **CI processor variants**: Won't do in MVP. The confusion matrix and drill logic work for any
   CI user. Processor-specific customization (Cochlear vs. MED-EL vs. Advanced Bionics channel
   mapping) is a potential future enhancement but not required for usefulness.

## Success Criteria

- Phase 1: Can play an interval on guitar and get accurate feedback within 1 second
- Phase 1: After 10 sessions, confusion matrix shows which interval+register combinations are hardest
- Phase 1: "Drill My Misses" produces a session focused on the user's specific weak spots
- Phase 1: CI keep-alive prevents Bluetooth streaming dropout across a full practice session
- Phase 2: Can play back a 4-note melody and get note-by-note accuracy feedback
